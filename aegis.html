This embedded preview uses the same demonstration scenarios and decision logic documented in the working Aegis prototype. The full application is implemented in FastAPI/Python with a local policy knowledge base, TF-IDF retrieval, scenario-aware reranking, governance guardrails, and optional Claude grounded synthesis.

Why I built it

To explore governance as part of the architecture, not an afterthought.

Aegis uses a fictional law-firm setting because it creates a useful test case for high-stakes knowledge work. The purpose is not legal automation. The purpose is to demonstrate how an AI assistant can be constrained by a defined knowledge base, required to cite supporting material, refuse questions outside its boundaries, and separate language-model synthesis from deterministic risk and action rules.

Grounded responses

The assistant is designed around retrieval from an approved knowledge base rather than unconstrained generation.

Human-in-the-loop design

The prototype assumes AI supports professional judgment rather than replacing consequential human decisions.

Knowledge boundaries

Requests outside the defined source set are refused instead of answered from general model knowledge.

Policy hierarchy

Primary and supporting sources are distinguished so the response structure reflects the authority of the underlying material.

Guardrails outside generation

Risk and action logic is deterministic and separate from model-generated prose, reducing dependence on the model for control decisions.

Deployment-minded controls

The portfolio version includes rate limiting, a question-length limit, server-side API key handling, and a health endpoint.

Design model

A simple governance pathway

1. RetrieveUse approved source material relevant to the request.
2. GroundConstrain synthesis to the retrieved evidence.
3. CiteShow the policy or source basis supporting the response.
4. GuardApply deterministic risk and action rules outside model generation.
5. DeferKeep final consequential judgment with a human.
Institutional relevance: Aegis demonstrates the questions I believe institutions should ask before scaling AI: What knowledge is the system allowed to use? How is evidence surfaced? What decisions remain human? Where are risk controls enforced? What happens when the system does not know?

What this project demonstrates

This prototype is evidence of hands-on AI fluency used to inform broader leadership questions about governance, trustworthy implementation, human oversight, and responsible institutional adoption.
