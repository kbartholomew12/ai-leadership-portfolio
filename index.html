Leadership approach

From experimentation to institutional practice.

These projects reflect hands-on experimentation used to inform broader decisions about responsible AI adoption, faculty readiness, curriculum design, accessibility, assessment, academic quality, and organizational change. Effective AI leadership requires enough practical fluency to understand the tools, enough academic judgment to recognize their limits, and enough organizational experience to help people adopt new practices successfully.

My work is grounded in more than 25 years in higher education and more than a decade of academic leadership. As Assistant Dean of Biological Sciences, I lead faculty operations and instructional quality for approximately 60–65 part-time faculty across Biology, Environmental Science, and Nutrition.

What responsible adoption requires

  • Human judgment in consequential academic decisions
  • Clear learning and institutional goals before tool selection
  • Intentional review of accuracy, accessibility, integrity, and risk
  • Faculty support that moves beyond awareness to confident practice
  • Governance, measurement, and feedback strong enough to support scale

From prototype to scale

A successful prototype is the beginning of the work, not the end.

Moving emerging technology into sustainable institutional practice requires a deliberate pathway from exploration to evaluation, faculty enablement, governance, scale, and continuous improvement.

01

Explore

Understand capabilities through hands-on experimentation and real academic use cases.

02

Evaluate

Review value, accuracy, accessibility, ethics, risk, and the role of human judgment.

03

Enable

Translate successful approaches into faculty development, practical guidance, templates, and support.

04

Scale

Build shared standards, workflows, governance, and reusable systems across contexts.

05

Improve

Use outcomes, feedback, implementation barriers, and new capabilities to refine the system over time.
