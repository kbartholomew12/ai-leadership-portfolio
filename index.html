AI Leadership & Learning Innovation

I work at the intersection of academic leadership, faculty development, curriculum transformation, and emerging technology. My focus is not AI for its own sake. Leadership approach

From experimentation to institutional practice.

These projects reflect hands-on experimentation used to inform broader decisions about responsible AI adoption, faculty readiness, curriculum design, accessibility, assessment, and academic quality. I believe effective AI leadership requires enough practical fluency to understand the tools, enough academic judgment to recognize their limits, and enough organizational experience to help people adopt new practices successfully.

My work is grounded in more than 25 years in higher education and more than a decade of academic leadership. As Assistant Dean of Biological Sciences, I lead faculty operations and instructional quality for approximately 60–65 part-time faculty across Biology, Environmental Science, and Nutrition.

What responsible adoption requires

  • Human judgment remains part of consequential academic decisions.
  • Learning outcomes come before the novelty of the technology.
  • Accuracy, accessibility, integrity, and usability are reviewed intentionally.
  • Faculty need practical support, not simply access to tools.
  • Innovation should produce evidence that can guide improvement and scale.
Selected work

Applied AI and learning innovation

The examples below show how I explore emerging capabilities through real instructional problems while keeping the larger institutional questions in view.

Interactive learning

Protein Journey

An illustrated, continuously animated learning experience for undergraduate nutrition students that follows protein digestion, absorption, transport, and utilization. The project combines scientific review, narration support, embedded questioning, playback controls, and browser-based delivery.

Why it matters institutionally: Demonstrates how AI-supported prototyping can accelerate development of high-quality digital learning while faculty retain responsibility for disciplinary accuracy, accessibility, instructional sequencing, and final quality decisions.

Faculty readiness

Generative & Agentic AI Faculty Development

Faculty-facing exploration of generative AI, agentic workflows, authentic assessment, and rapid prototyping for learning. The work focuses on helping instructors move beyond awareness toward purposeful experimentation and informed classroom use.

Why it matters institutionally: AI adoption succeeds when professional learning connects emerging tools to faculty practice, disciplinary judgment, academic integrity, and clear expectations for use.
Authentic assessment

Assessment Redesign in an AI Environment

A continuing body of work focused on designing assessments that require application, explanation, decision-making, and evidence of student reasoning rather than relying primarily on tasks that generative AI can complete for students.

Why it matters institutionally: Responsible AI strategy cannot stop at tool adoption. Institutions also need assessment practices that preserve meaningful evidence of learning as student access to generative AI expands.
Scalable learning systems

Interactive Science Prototypes

Browser-based tutorials, simulations, scenarios, and interactive learning experiences developed through AI-assisted workflows and then reviewed for scientific accuracy, usability, accessibility, and alignment with course outcomes.

Why it matters institutionally: Lightweight prototyping can help faculty test ideas quickly, identify what creates learning value, and develop reusable models before investing in broader implementation.
From prototype to scale

Innovation becomes valuable when people can actually use it.

My leadership approach treats a successful prototype as the beginning of the work, not the end. Moving innovation into sustainable practice requires a deliberate pathway from exploration to adoption, governance, measurement, and continuous improvement.

01

Explore

Understand emerging capabilities through hands-on experimentation and real academic use cases.

02

Evaluate

Review learning value, accuracy, accessibility, ethics, risk, and the role of human judgment.

03

Enable

Translate successful approaches into faculty development, practical guidance, templates, and support.

04

Scale

Build shared standards, workflows, governance, and reusable systems that work across people and contexts.

05

Improve

Use outcomes, feedback, implementation barriers, and new capabilities to refine the system over time.
